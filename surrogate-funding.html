Our mission

We stand at the crossroads.

Surrogate Funding stands at the crossroads for young men. We mentor them so they see the trap before they step in it. And when a man is locked up over a few hundred dollars, we bring him home.

Our vision. Every young man in the Midlands has a man in his corner who tells him the truth before the streets, the wrong friends, or the system do.

The founder's story

Wounded in the house of a friend.

After Zechariah 13:6

I came up in the projects. At 21, I built a house from the ground up in a neighborhood where my neighbors were retired military men. I thought I was doing everything right. I had a mentor, a man I prayed with and broke bread with. Then someone lied on me, and he believed it without ever asking me. I had to prove my own innocence before he would even call me back.

That is when I learned what Zechariah meant by being wounded in the house of a friend. Nobody taught me how to see betrayal coming, how to choose my circle, or how to protect what I was building. I learned it the hard way.

I started Surrogate Funding so young men learn it from someone who has been there, not from a courtroom or a cell. Faith, family, finances, and order. Loud and right.

Lewis BroomeFounder

The bonds that keep people in jail

No one should lose a job, a home, or their children over a few hundred dollars.

One man sat in jail for three months because he couldn't pay a $70 bond. He is not the only one. While people wait, the job goes, the rent goes, and sometimes their children go with it.

1We post small bonds for people held only because they cannot pay.
2They go home to their families and keep their court dates.
3When the case ends, the refunded bail returns to the fund and brings the next person home.

Inside, right now

The sink

In some cells the sink barely runs. A man can't wash his face without laying his rag on the steel edge where every other man's rag has been. People wait in conditions like that for days over a bond smaller than a phone bill.

The father

Some are fathers held over child support who can't raise a $300 bond. While they sit, the arrears keep growing. They lose the job that paid the support, and no one hires them after. It becomes almost a life sentence.

The old man in D block

In one county jail, an older man with dementia kept trying to walk out of his block into sections where he wasn't safe. He didn't know he was in jail. The men around him had to explain that he couldn't help himself.

We are not against the system. We are building a better one, where no one loses their life over a few hundred dollars.
