The bonds that keep people in jail

No one should lose a job, a home, or their children over a few hundred dollars.

One man sat in jail for three months because he couldn't pay a $70 bond. He is not the only one. While people wait, the job goes, the rent goes, and sometimes their children go with it.

Inside, right now

The sink

In some cells the sink barely runs. A man can't wash his face without laying his rag on the steel edge where every other man's rag has been. People wait in conditions like that for days over a bond smaller than a phone bill.

The father

Some are fathers held over child support who can't raise a $300 bond. While they sit, the arrears keep growing. They lose the job that paid the support, and no one hires them after. It becomes almost a life sentence.

The old man in D block

In one county jail, an older man with dementia kept trying to walk out of his block into sections where he wasn't safe. He didn't know he was in jail. The men around him had to explain that he couldn't help himself.

We are not against the system. We are building a better one, where no one loses their life over a few hundred dollars.

If our fund can't help yet

Well-rated bonding companies, by county.

A bonding company posts the full bond for a fee, called the premium, which you do not get back. Many offer payment plans with a small down payment, and the down payment is often negotiable. Ask. Our fund is different: we post the bond ourselves, and you owe us nothing.
