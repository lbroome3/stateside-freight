🇨🇦 Canada

Freight moving north or south across the US-Canada border, by truckload or LTL.

Detroit – Windsor

The busiest commercial crossing on the northern border, for Michigan, the Midwest and the Ontario corridor.

Buffalo – Niagara

The gateway between the Northeast and the Toronto and Golden Horseshoe region.

Port Huron – Sarnia

A strong alternative to Detroit for Ontario freight, and often a smoother crossing when Detroit backs up.

LTL into Canada: send the pallet count, dimensions, weight and class, and the team quotes it the same way as a full truck.

🇲🇽 Mexico

More manufacturing is moving to Mexico, and the freight follows it. We move it both ways: parts and materials heading south, finished goods coming north.

Laredo first

The largest truck gateway between the US and Mexico, and the starting point for most of our Mexico lanes.

El Paso

For the Ciudad Juárez manufacturing corridor and the western half of the border.

Otay Mesa

The San Diego – Tijuana crossing for West Coast and Baja California freight.

Most Mexico freight changes hands at the border: a US truck to the border, a transfer across, and a Mexican carrier on the other side. We line up each leg so you deal with one team.

How it works

  1. Tell us the lane. Origin, destination, freight, size and pickup date, using the form below or a call.
  2. We plan the crossing. The right border point, equipment and timing for your freight, with a quote in writing.
  3. Your broker gets the paperwork. We coordinate with your customs broker on each side so the entry is ready before the truck reaches the border.
  4. We track it door to door. One team watches the load from pickup to delivery and keeps you posted.

What you need for a clean crossing

  • Commercial invoice: what is in the shipment, its value and who is buying and selling.
  • Packing list: pieces, weights and how it is packed.
  • Your customs broker, on each side of the border. No broker yet? Tell us and we will point you to licensed brokers.
  • For Mexico, the Mexican documents your broker prepares (such as the pedimento and the Carta Porte) travel with the load.
  • USMCA certification, if your goods qualify and you are claiming it. Your broker can tell you.

Do you clear customs?

Your licensed customs broker clears the freight on each side. We coordinate the truck, the timing and the paperwork hand-off with them.

Truckload or LTL?

Both. Full truckloads (dry van, flatbed, reefer and more) and LTL pallets into and out of Canada and Mexico.

How long does a crossing take?

It depends on the crossing, the time of day and whether the paperwork is ready ahead of time. Having your broker's entry ready before the truck arrives makes the biggest difference.

Who hauls the freight?

Owner-operators on Landstar's network in the US, with partner carriers for the Mexican leg where the lane needs it.
